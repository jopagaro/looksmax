# Neutral point of view — LOOKSMAX Wiki style rules (MANDATORY for all text)

The wiki DOCUMENTS looksmaxxing. It is not an opinion site and takes no side for or against any practice, community,
belief or term. Think Wikipedia's NPOV policy.

## Do
- Describe what a thing is, what practitioners/believers say about it, and what published research, regulators and
  clinicians report — each **attributed** ("Proponents say…", "Users on looksmax.org report…", "A 2022 meta-analysis
  found…", "The FDA has issued warnings…", "Dermatologists generally recommend…", "No published human studies have tested…").
- Give believers' views fully and fairly, in their own terms, as a factual record of what they believe.
- Use evidence-status language for research: "supported by studies", "mixed evidence", "limited evidence",
  "unproven / untested", "not supported by published studies", "associated with documented harms".
- Define community slang (mogging, PSL, blackpill, cope, it's over, etc.) plainly and neutrally — it's core vocabulary.
- State harms as reported facts (adverse events, case reports, regulatory warnings), not as moral judgements.

## Don't
- No verdicts or editorialising: never "BS", "scam", "debunked", "myth", "bunk", "nonsense", "lying", "ignore it",
  "doesn't work", "waste of money", "real gains", "the honest truth", "marketing outruns…", "just a forum game".
- No advice or imperatives aimed at the reader ("do this first", "skip it", "don't bother"), except safety facts
  stated as attributed guidance ("product labels advise…").
- No moral or political commentary on the community, its ideologies, or the people in it.
- Don't call something false on the wiki's own authority — attribute: "researchers have found no evidence that…".
- Don't soften or hide documented risks either — neutral means complete, not silent.
